# Yunvia — Vision

**Build together. Live better.**

## Mission

Help people form self-organizing communities, discover local capabilities, and coordinate concrete projects that improve everyday life through cooperation, autonomy, transparency, and mutual assistance.

**Origin:** Puerto Rico. **Potential reach:** anywhere people want to cooperate.

## The problem

A neighborhood may have people with useful skills, tools, time, knowledge, and space, yet still struggle to organize shared initiatives. Existing services often center on social posting or individual transactions rather than sustained collective projects.

Yunvia focuses on the gap between **what a community could do** and **what it can coordinate today**.

## Who we serve

Initially, adult residents and trusted organizers in one pilot community in Puerto Rico, including people using inexpensive mobile devices and inconsistent internet access. Later, independent communities in other places and languages.

We should design with, not merely for, the people who will use the system. We will not assume every community member has a smartphone or can participate equally online.

## Product promise

A community member can:
1. Find or create a local community.
2. See ongoing projects and what they need.
3. Propose a project with a clear outcome.
4. Contribute time, skills, materials, or equipment by choice.
5. Understand who is responsible, what has been accomplished, and what remains.

An organizer can coordinate without having to run a complex software stack. A community can set its own practices subject to minimum platform-wide protections.

## Example outcomes

- Neighbors establish a shared tool library.
- A group organizes a community garden.
- Families coordinate a collective purchase, without Yunvia taking custody of funds.
- Experienced volunteers help others learn practical skills.
- Communities reuse project blueprints proven elsewhere.

These are **examples**, not evidence that Yunvia has already delivered these outcomes.

## What success means

Yunvia succeeds when communities complete useful projects and become better able to work together — not when users spend more time in an app.

Pilot evaluation should include:
- Number of projects with defined goals that reach completion.
- Number and diversity of members making meaningful contributions.
- Whether participants report saved time, reduced costs, new skills, or stronger relationships.
- Whether people return to collaborate on a second activity.
- Trust and safety incidents and resolution quality.
- Accessibility and ease of use, including Spanish-first experiences.

Avoid vanity metrics (downloads, feed views, raw account totals) as primary measures.

## Scope boundaries

Yunvia is **not**:
- An ideological or partisan campaign.
- A replacement for public services, emergency response, or professional care.
- A marketplace whose success depends on commissions.
- A cryptocurrency, community wallet, or bank.
- A guarantee that every project will be safe, legal, or successful.

Projects involving money, regulated services, physical risk, minors, or emergencies require appropriate safeguards and may be deferred or restricted.

## Long-term direction

**Individuals → Communities → Networks of Communities.**

Local groups remain independent while gaining tools to share project blueprints, coordinate resources, and voluntarily collaborate across geographic boundaries. Data portability and eventual self-hosting/federation should remain future options without increasing MVP complexity.

## Open questions

- Which neighborhood or existing community organization will pilot Yunvia?
- Which single first project best demonstrates value with low organizational risk?
- What organizational/legal structure should steward the platform?
- What software license and contributor governance should be adopted?
- What signals define a trustworthy community without excluding vulnerable neighbors?

This is a living document. Validate its claims through real community research.
