# Yunvia — Principles

These principles guide product, governance, moderation, and technical decisions. They are **project commitments**, not a claim that legal governance structures have already been established.

## 1. Cooperation

Enable meaningful projects with goals, responsibilities, milestones, and voluntary contributions. Prioritize completed community outcomes over activity feeds and engagement tricks.

## 2. Autonomy

Communities should choose their membership processes, projects, and internal coordination methods, within minimum platform-wide protections. Members can leave. Individuals retain control over what they offer and what they disclose.

**Autonomy is not unrestricted administrator power.** We will design for checks on coordinator decisions and an appeal path for significant enforcement decisions.

## 3. Transparency

Clearly explain project status, responsibilities, moderation rules, and product decisions. Transparency about public community matters must **not** expose private member information. Keep access to sensitive records limited and auditable.

## 4. Mutual assistance

Celebrate sharing, teaching, lending, and volunteering without requiring every contribution to be a reciprocal transaction. Avoid ranking people by financial value, hours, or perceived worth.

## 5. Accessibility

Build Spanish- and English-language experiences from the beginning, with Spanish-first usability for the Puerto Rico pilot. Support screen readers, keyboard navigation, legible text, plain language, low-end devices, and slow connections. Research accessibility with actual users; do not treat translation alone as inclusion.

## 6. Nonpartisanship and civic freedom

The **platform** does not endorse political parties or candidates. Communities may pursue lawful civic initiatives and debate public issues. Enforce behavior-based rules against threats, harassment, discrimination, fraud, and abuse rather than suppressing lawful viewpoints solely because they are political.

The platform's neutrality is not neutrality toward harmful conduct.

## 7. Privacy and data minimization

Collect only information needed to coordinate. Do not display exact home addresses or precise live locations by default. Do not sell personal data or fund the service through behavioral advertising. Provide sensible audience settings, data export pathways, and account deletion processes, with clearly disclosed retention limits where legally or operationally necessary.

## 8. Safety and accountability

Physical-world cooperation introduces risks. At minimum:
- Report and block harmful behavior.
- Separate community coordinator powers from platform safety enforcement.
- Restrict access to personal and sensitive data.
- Establish transparent escalation and appeal processes.
- Use least-privilege authorization; never trust a UI-only permission check.
- Be cautious with tools, transportation, home visits, emergency requests, regulated work, and projects involving minors.

Do **not** imply that volunteer roles or ratings guarantee safety.

## 9. Financial restraint

In the initial product, coordinate needs and pledges, **not custody, escrow, wallets, or automatic redistribution of money**. No mandatory transaction commissions. Structured time credits, money flows, and tax-related features require further legal and product analysis.

## 10. Open development and portability

Develop in public where practical and adopt an explicit open-source license **before accepting external code contributions**. Seek community feedback on governance. Design for exportable project/community data and avoid unnecessary vendor lock-in. Self-hosting and federation may come later; do not misrepresent them as launch capabilities.

## Decision-making approach

For a contested feature, ask:
1. Does it help people accomplish a useful project together?
2. Does it protect participant autonomy and personal information?
3. Can people understand who makes decisions and why?
4. Does it work for low-resource and less technical participants?
5. Can abuse be detected, reported, and addressed?
6. Can a simpler implementation validate the same value first?

When principles collide (for example, transparency versus privacy), document the tradeoff rather than treating either as absolute.

## Governance: provisional starting model

For the pilot, use **member / coordinator / platform moderator** roles:
- **Members** can participate in projects and manage their own offers and contributions.
- **Coordinators** can help manage community membership and projects, with logged consequential actions.
- **Platform moderators** can investigate platform-wide safety concerns under written, reviewable rules.

Voting systems, formal elections, recall procedures, and multiple governance models are **not yet designed**. Preserve flexibility in the authorization and audit architecture rather than promising functionality not built.
